Part IIIWhere this goesThe promises, the economics, and the plan to compound.

Sixty days in, the emergency is becoming an operation: three straight weeks at peak volume with answers in minutes, backlogs burned down, the anchor book at target throughout. Handling today's demand is approaching steady state — which means attention now shifts from coping to compounding.

In support, the experience curve and the cost curve are the same curve. A blocker cleared before the call is the best possible experience and the cheapest contact. We never trade quality against cost — every lever pulls both.

Sixty days of instrumentation produced more than visibility — increasingly, we know why a patient is about to contact us before they do. And automation turns that knowledge into capacity: every transactional contact the AI absorbs releases people to the work only humans can do — walking a stalled patient through their labs, saving a cancellation, reaching out before the blocker bites. The machines take the routine so our people can take the relationship.

The stakes are bigger than a support budget: launches, marketing spend, and client promises scale only as fast as support absorbs them. A support org that can't becomes the company's growth constraint. One that can becomes the reason we say yes to bigger launches.

The vision

Six promises to every patient

Six measurable commitments — not adjectives — in the order a patient experiences them: the best contact never has to happen, and every promise after is the graceful fallback.

P1We reach you before you need us A guided journey rather than a help desk: AI watches every patient's progress and flags who's stalled or about to be. Blockers clear automatically where they can; where they can't, a named human walks the patient through labs, visits, and refills until they're moving again. 20–30% of would-be contacts pre-empted; no patient stalls unseen.
P2Whatever channel you choose works When you do need us: full parity — voice, chat, SMS, email, portal — on every brand, 24/7, AI-first with graceful human handoff.
P3We answer fast Voice 90% ≤30s · chat 90% ≤30s · email first response <1hr — attained every week, on every brand. Plus journey standards: new patients contacted, and missed calls returned, within 24 hours.
P4We already know why you're reaching out Blockers, journey stage, order state, and history converge so contacts open with the answer already in hand — and with the right value conversation when one exists. Reason anticipated on 70%+ of contacts.
P5You never repeat yourself First-contact resolution 75%+ (from ~58–60% today, vs ~70% industry) — zero cold transfers, context carried across every channel and handoff.
P6Every interaction makes us better 100% of interactions QA-scored by AI, closed-loop into coaching, prompts, and product.
The signature capability is P4 — answering the phone already knowing. The raw material is live (intent captured on ~85% of Embody and AltRx calls, joined to journey and order state), and it compounds every other promise: shorter handles, higher first-contact resolution, the data behind proactive outreach.
The market proof. A best-in-class cash-pay telehealth operator demonstrates this thesis at boutique scale: support-led experience producing 88% one-year retention, 89% repeat-customer revenue (industry ~60%), and ~a third of new patients arriving by referral — growth on 1.2% marketing spend at a $162 CAC. Their playbook — journey shepherds at every stage, a dedicated winback team, pre-ship order QA, 24-hour SLAs, staff AI, patient risk-scoring — maps promise-for-promise onto this plan. We run ~25× their volume with deeper data. Done right, support doesn't just keep patients — it acquires them.
The voice of the patient. Support heard roughly a million patient conversations in August alone — the largest listening post in the company, and historically its least used. Every contact lands as structured signal — reason, sentiment, journey stage, cancellation cause — that already reshapes self-service flows and has surfaced upstream billing defects worth millions. At 100% QA the loop closes: support findings fix product, funnel, and pricing, removing the causes of contacts. The best support call is the one product never lets happen.

The economics

Support that scales sub-linearly with growth

The financial plan already assumes support gets dramatically more efficient as the company scales. These are the levers that deliver it.

  • Automate the routine — worth ~$10M/yr versus a no-AI operation at today's demand: channel deflection run to its measured ceilings, Level AI effects, and back-office automation — scaling with growth.
  • Staffing intensity falls ~30% as the AI ramps mature: once deflection, Level AI, and back-office automation reach their modeled ceilings (mid-2027), serving today's book takes roughly 450 fewer FTE-equivalents than it does today (~76k paid vendor hours a month, front-line pool and back-office block both). Whether that lands as fewer heads or as growth absorbed without hiring is the growth plan's call; either way, headcount stops scaling with volume.
  • Buy at should-cost: spend runs ~$1.0M a month (32%) above the computed should-cost — invoice-tied in July, and the gap survives pricing the back-office block on its own drivers. The recovery instrument is the contract-by-contract vendor review now sequencing: renewals repriced against measured work, structured on mutual incentives, with volume following scorecard performance.
The revenue engine

Six lanes from cost line to revenue line

Support touches ~250,000 patients a month on a book that bills ~$1.7B a year — a single percentage point of that book is ~$17M a year, and no function stands closer to it. The market pays asymmetrically here: winning back a lapsed customer succeeds at 60–70% vs 5–20% for a new one, and retention costs 5–25× less than acquisition. The lanes are sequenced so the measured fund the ambitious.

R1Protect — the save desk Cancel-intent contacts handled as revenue moments — reason-coded save plays (the offer differs by why they're leaving), with root-cause fixes in the agent's hand. Every retained patient-month is $252 at 73% gross margin (measured). Conservative modeled floor: $2.2M/yr.
R2Reactivate — winback The highest-odds sale in commerce — relaunched now, with a four-client pilot showing the ceiling: 59% of patients worked, saved (29% conservative). At the measured $252/patient-month, every 1,000 patients worked per month recovers ~$2.6–5.3M/yr — staffed by ~3 agents, scaling one agent at a time. Low risk, modeled staffing, proven ceiling.
R3Prevent — proactive revenue defense Promise P1, monetized: blockers cleared before subscriptions stall. Only 13% of customers anywhere ever receive proactive service (Gartner), and it lifts NPS, satisfaction, and effort by a full point. Near-total whitespace — and we hold the data to own it.
R4Recover — refund & credit integrity Pre-ship checks and policy discipline against the ~$5M/yr of refund leakage already surfaced — first prevention tranche conservatively modeled at ~$0.3M/yr, with the upstream defects fixed at the source rather than absorbed as goodwill.
R5Grow — cross-sell & upsell Every service contact is a trusted moment with a decision-ready patient: the refill, the plan question, the milestone. With reason and journey known (P4), those become client-approved value conversations: plan upgrades at the refill moment, adjacent-program cross-sell on the same book, education-led expansion, with the next-best-offer on the agent's screen. The same 60–70% existing-customer odds winback exploits, applied before anyone drifts — sold to clients as a managed growth channel.
R6Compound — referrals & premium tiers Referred patients carry 16% higher lifetime value and 25% higher initial margins (Wharton, Journal of Marketing) — best-in-class operators book a third of new patients this way. On top: premium support tiers clients pay for, and patient-insight reporting packaged into client partnerships.
The endgame — support as a profit center: PS-attributed revenue covers the cost of running PS, then clears it. Break-even is the milestone, not the destination — support becomes a P&L line with a plus sign, and something clients pay for. R1 and R4 are measured and fundable this quarter; R2 and R3 ride the proactive machinery of Phase 2; R5 rides the context engine as it lands; R6 is the Phase-3 product.

Method notes

Measured means computed from our own systems of record — contact-center trace records, ticketing and billing reconciliations, contract documents — not vendor claims or industry rules of thumb. Modeled means derived from the demand-driven cost model (patients → contacts by channel → handle time → paid hours → cost), which reconciles against actual P&L monthly; deflection is always counted net of re-contacts, and should-cost is priced at planning-standard occupancy and shrinkage. Where an assumption is chosen rather than measured, it is chosen conservatively — retention economics use a fraction of published churn-attribution benchmarks. 2027 figures scale with the finance growth plan once it lands; they are stated at today's demand. Winback economics use our own 2025 four-client pilot (731 lapsed patients worked, 80% contact rate; 59% blended save rate, 29% on the conservative cut excluding the largest pilot client) priced at the measured $252/patient-month with the standard three-month tail. The motion has since relaunched; pilot rates are directional until the relaunch produces its own read, and the identified lapsed-patient flow's true monthly size is under review — so sizing is stated per 1,000 patients worked. Revenue-lane external evidence: the Wharton/Goethe referral-value study (Journal of Marketing, 2011: +16% LTV, +25% initial margin); published win-back research (60–70% re-sell odds vs 5–20% new); Gartner customer-service research (13% proactive-service incidence; full-point NPS/CSAT/CES lift); Bain/HBR retention economics (5% retention → 25–95% profit; acquisition 5–25× retention cost). Internal figures — $252/patient-month, 73% gross margin, refund leakage — are measured on our own systems. The $6.16 unit cost is modeled, not measured: August all-in should-cost (cost model v1.7, including the 390-head back-office block) ÷ the June active-subscriber base; the chat-demand base and the subscriber denominator refresh when finance's patient plan lands, and the trajectory ($6.16 → $5.20 → ~$4.00) is sturdier than the level since all three points share the same basis. Materials circulated earlier quoted $5.28 → $3.00 on the same construction before the back-office block was costed in — the block ($389k/mo ÷ the June base = $0.88) is the entire difference in the today figure. The vendor premium, by contrast, is now measured: July invoices normalized and tied out against the model. The deflection chart's email row is the Code Yellow team's own counter (the "HeyRevia Closed Time" field): 17.5–22.7k tickets a week closed by HeyRevia in late August. It counts closes without a human reply — verified end-to-end AI answers peaked at ~7.7k a week, with the balance no-reply resolutions (duplicates, holds, already-resolved elsewhere). The hero's ~40% is the cost model's coefficient, measured 8/31 directly as 1 − the human-answered share of ~44.5k/week inbound patient email; the counter and the coefficient land in the same range by independent methods.
